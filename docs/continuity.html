Continuity

Data ranges

Date ranges of datasets differ. Hemberger & Williams (2025) collected data at 5 timepoints from 2003-02-20 to 2003-07-30. Guezen and Forrest (2021) collected data from 4 timepoints from 2016-05-20 to 2016-09-01.

Since Iverson has estimates for every day, I limit continuity calculation from 2016-03-01 to 2016-09-30, which is when there are significant flowers predicted. As an alternative, to see if it matters, I also tried sampling midway through each month in the full range (7 months), and for a truncated range of 2016-04-15 to 2016-08-15 (5 months) that matches the other studies’ dates better.

Continuity distributions

For Aaron’s data, I wanted to compare differences in coefficient of variation between the full range of dates and the subsampled dates. There is significant correlation between the full range and sampled dates. If 7 sample dates are used, then the scatter of points in this comparison is around the 1:1 line (fig below). But if 5 sample dates are used to sample, the CV values estimated from 5 samples (orange points, below) are lower than the CV estimated from 7 samples.

Comparison of distributions

Site continuity values by study. Hemberger & Williams (2025) has 5 timepoints, Guezen & Forrest (2021) has 4 timepoints, and three versions of CV calculated for Aaron’s data using 5 timepoints, 7 timepoints, and 214 timepoints (daily values).

Another way to measure continuity is stability, which is 1/cv. This feels a little more intuitive to me because higher values correspond to higher stability, which I’m interpreting as continuity.

It seems like the data would be more comparable if we limited Aaron’s data to 5 samples. The CV values and stability values are in a similar range to the other datasets.

The relationship between % natural and crop shannon diversity / crop richness

There’s an upper limit to crop diversity/richness in the plots below, which decreases as % natural reaches 100%. I believe this is determined in part by field size. You can only have one crop per field, and you can only have so many fields as crop area decreases. So I think that’s what’s determining the upper limit to Aaron’s data in the diversity and richness plots below.

Exploratory models

I fit the following models and then compare their AIC. A three-way interaction is most supported, which suggests the slopes between % natural and crop richness interact, and the relationships change between studies.

stability ~ pct.natural + crop.rich + study 
AIC: 
508.1707 
 
stability ~ pct.natural * crop.rich * study 
AIC: 
506.2563 
 
stability ~ (pct.natural + crop.rich) * study 
AIC: 
513.6499

These changes seem to be mainly driven by a strong interaction between % natural and crop richness for the Guezen & Forrest (2021) study. Slopes mostly aren’t significantly different from zero across these variables’ interactions for the other two studies.
